WEEK 4 Professional Growth October 1, 2026

Learning to Communicate More Intentionally

One thing I have been thinking about this semester is how much effective communication depends on both the audience and the quality of the reasoning behind the message.

In my MCOM class, we have discussed different logical fallacies, including ideas like bandwagon reasoning and appeals to authority. Learning to recognize these has made me more aware of how easy it can be for an argument to sound convincing without actually being well supported.

It has also made me think more carefully about the way I communicate my own ideas. I want to make sure that when I make a recommendation or explain something, I am not just relying on what sounds persuasive, but that I understand the reasoning and evidence behind it.

Between school and my internship, I also regularly communicate with people who have different levels of background knowledge, priorities, and expectations. I am becoming more intentional about deciding how much context someone needs, what information is most important, and how I can present it clearly without making it more complicated than necessary.

Organization is another major part of how I approach my work. I genuinely enjoy planning and consider it one of my stronger skills. I use different digital tools when they are helpful, but I still rely heavily on a physical planner. Writing out deadlines, meetings, priorities, and tasks helps me see everything in one place and think ahead.

These experiences are helping me see that communication, critical thinking, and organization are closely connected. Good communication depends on clear reasoning, and both are much easier when I have taken the time to prepare and understand what needs to happen next.

WEEK 3 Academic Learning September 24, 2026

Connecting Art History, Business, and Ethics

One of my favorite parts of school is finding connections between subjects that initially seem unrelated. This semester, I have been able to combine my interest in art history with what I'm learning in business ethics and professional communication.

For my MCOM class, I am researching a business article titled When Wealth Becomes a Moral Dilemma: Religious Patronage in Early Fourteenth-Century Italy . My working topic explores how Enrico Scrovegni's commission of Giotto's Arena Chapel reflects the moral tensions surrounding commercially acquired wealth in fourteenth-century Italy.

Through this project, I am learning how to narrow a broad topic into a focused research question, find and evaluate reliable academic sources, and connect ideas from multiple disciplines including art history, religion, and economic history.

I have also been using AI as a brainstorming and research-support tool. It has helped me think through possible directions and identify promising scholarly sources, while also reinforcing the importance of independently evaluating those sources before relying on them.

WEEK 2 Internship Learning September 19, 2026

Managing Work Across Teams

One of the biggest things I'm learning through my project management internship is that keeping a project moving often depends just as much on communication as it does on completing individual tasks.

I currently help coordinate 6–10 projects at a time, which means working across different teams, keeping track of changing priorities, following up with people, and making sure everyone has the information they need to keep moving forward.

This experience is helping me better understand the importance of clear communication, organization, and visibility when multiple people contribute to the same project.
